# Report 196: SSRM-3D Market Dispute Court, Public Law Memory, and Restorative Contract Repair Bridge

## Purpose

Report 196 extends the Report 195 guild marketplace with deterministic public dispute repair. The goal is not to create real law, real courts, real contracts, moral patienthood, subjective guilt, or subjective consciousness. The goal is a traceable artificial-life substrate where market obligations can break, be filed as public cases, receive evidence packets, be reviewed by an impartial panel, bind precedent memory, and close through restorative repair rather than silent reputation decay.

This is a social-institution bridge: contracts now have a public consequence path when fulfillment breaks.

## Implementation

The experiment is implemented in `experiments/ssrm_3d_market_dispute_court_public_law_repair_bridge.py`.

It consumes `artifacts/ssrm_3d_guild_marketplace_reciprocal_credit_contract_bridge_state.json` from Report 195 and adds:

- dispute case filing
- evidence packets from prior contracts
- impartial two-agent panel selection
- adjudication
- public law memory
- precedent binding
- restorative contract repair
- trust recovery
- contract fairness checks
- repeat-breach prevention
- appeal review
- obligation closure
- guild-market court memory binding
- frequency/flower court rhythm binding
- privacy-preserving browser replay packets

No LLMs are called. All outputs are deterministic for seed `20260809` and `7` court cycles.

## Interpretation

Report 195 made guild trade possible. Report 196 gives trade a repair institution.

The key shift is that failed or disputed obligations no longer remain private ledger noise. They become public cases with evidence, panel review, precedent, repair, closure, and memory. This makes the artificial society more legible and less brittle: bad contracts can be repaired without simply deleting trust or pretending every exchange succeeds.

The bridge also distinguishes ordinary non-dispute events from court failures. Non-dispute cycles are not counted as missing adjudication; they are counted as calibrated restraint. The court should activate when needed, not manufacture cases to inflate metrics.

## Browser replay

The browser visualization shows:

- per-cycle public case packets
- filing, adjudication, precedent, repair, appeal, and closure state
- credit and trust snapshots
- court memory counts
- frequency and flower-node bindings
- claim-boundary indicators

The replay is public-facing by design. Private workspace contents remain hidden when the privacy filter is active.

## Moral and claim boundary

The experiment explicitly keeps these boundaries:

- public law is not real law
- court repair is not a real legal remedy
- dispute state is not subjective guilt
- no subjective consciousness claim
- no moral patienthood claim
- private workspace is not debug-leaked

The point is to build inspectable social repair substrate, not to claim that the agents are legal persons or conscious subjects.

## Limitations

- There is no real legal authority.
- There is no real money or enforceable contract.
- The appeals process is shallow.
- Repeat-breach prevention is weak over the short run.
- The court panel is deterministic and small.
- The public-law memory is simple precedent, not a full norm system.
- The browser visualization is a replay surface, not complete 3D gameplay.

## Next gate

The next gate should be rights charters, consent norms, and public moral-boundary law for avatar interaction.

Market repair now exists. The next question is what agents are allowed to refuse, what avatar actions are socially prohibited, and how public norms protect boundaries without pretending to create real rights-bearing beings.
